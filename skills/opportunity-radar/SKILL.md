# Notice a useful opening

Retrieve the relevant owner idea and its current goal, dated evolution,
implementation, blockers and active ownership. Do not infer that old or quiet
work was abandoned. Keep the owner's causal reason for the idea, not only its
project name. Read [continuity](../../references/continuity.md).

Verify the external change from primary announcements, papers, repositories or
releases. Record event and publication dates, actual availability, source URL
and check time. Secondary coverage can suggest attention; popularity or a
headline cannot establish readiness, usefulness, originality or future virality.
Do not send private archive fragments as public search queries.

An actionable connection requires:

- a verified change and what it actually enables;
- an evidenced owner goal or idea that it materially helps;
- current assets, unresolved blockers and what changed about them;
- one feasible action and any real cost of delay;
- a useful delivery moment, considering the work it would interrupt.

Separate confirmed facts from the fit inference and proposed action. If the
connection lacks readiness or a feasible next action, retain a watch item with a
clear condition that would change the decision. Do not manufacture urgency or
recommend a rushed release that violates the owner's proof requirements.
An actionable item without a deadline can belong in the next requested briefing;
urgency decides interruption timing, not whether the connection is useful.

Deliver task-relevant context at the current decision or a natural completion
boundary. Batch unrelated nonurgent discoveries. A window switch, silence or
keyboard activity is not reliable evidence that the owner is available. If no
attention signal exists, deliver on explicit request or the configured briefing
schedule and label that limitation privately.

Deduplicate by owner idea, verified signal and meaningful action/state change.
Keep stable records; unchanged signals do not earn another alert. Mark delivery
only after the user-facing output or tool confirms it. Preserve a correction or
dismissal, with its source and scope, for the next match.

For an explicitly recurring request, inspect existing app automations before
creating one and prefer its supported thread heartbeat. Honor project-specific
no-automation constraints; do not monitor those projects through a wrapper.
Record cadence, scope and operational limitations. Creating a schedule does not
prove a scheduled run occurred, a notification was delivered, or future checks
will be uninterrupted. Never publish, contact people or redirect another task
as a side effect of monitoring.

Output a short useful connection and next action when the gate holds. Otherwise
produce a watch result only when requested; a routine monitoring run should
remain quiet when there is no new qualifying decision.
